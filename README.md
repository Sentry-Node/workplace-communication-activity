🛡️ Threat Glossary

Phishing: A type of social engineering attack where an attacker sends a fraudulent message designed to trick a person into revealing sensitive information or deploying malicious software.

Phishing Detection Indicators (The "Red Flags")
Sense of Urgency or Fear: Attackers often use high-pressure language to make you act without thinking.

Example: "Your account will be suspended in 2 hours," or "Urgent: Payment Overdue."

Suspicious Sender Address: The "From" name may look legitimate, but the actual email address is slightly altered or from a completely different domain.

Check: Does the domain match the official company website exactly (e.g., @company.com vs. @co-mpany.com)?

Generic Greetings: Professional organizations you have a relationship with usually address you by name. Be wary of "Dear Customer," "Valued Employee," or "Dear Member."

Poor Grammar and Layout: While some attacks are sophisticated, many contain spelling errors, awkward phrasing, or low-resolution logos that look "off."

Mismatched Hyperlinks: If you hover your mouse over a link without clicking, the actual destination URL will appear. If the hovered link doesn't match the text or the expected website, it is likely malicious.

Unusual Attachments: Be extremely cautious with unexpected files, especially those with unusual extensions (e.g., .zip, .exe, or .scr) or even common office files like Excel documents that prompt you to "Enable Macros."

Requests for Sensitive Data: Legitimate companies—especially IT or HR departments—will rarely ask for your password, credit card number, or MFA code via email.

Quick Verification Tip: The "Stop, Look, Think" Rule
If an email feels "off" or makes you feel panicked, stop. Instead of clicking links or downloading attachments, verify the request through a secondary, trusted channel—such as calling the colleague directly or visiting the official website by typing the address manually into your browser.

Security Protocol: If you suspect a message is a phishing attempt, do not click any links or download attachments. Immediately use the "Report Phish" button or forward the headers to the SOC (Security Operations Center).
